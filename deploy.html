Government deployment

Deploy EDIS across your institution

EDIS is deployed in stages. An institution can begin with a single analyst and grow to a national platform without changing tools, retraining its staff on something else or rebuilding its data integrations. Each stage adds users, models, countries, data and governance — the analytical infrastructure underneath stays the same.

Country configuration

EDIS is configured to a country, not pointed at one

A deployment is not a login to a generic model. The models are calibrated to the country’s own accounts, its own survey data, its own administrative registers and its own planning documents, and every result then carries that provenance.

Configuration covers the national data a country already holds; where a dataset does not exist, EDIS states the gap rather than filling it with an assumption.

What a country configuration contains Data and provenance

Configured for each country

  • National accounts, the macro-fiscal frame and the budget classification
  • Social accounting matrix and input–output structure
  • Household survey data for distributional analysis
  • Sector registers: facilities, schools, networks, generation, land
  • Unit costs and service norms
  • Spatial layers: population, settlements, infrastructure, hazard
  • Public investment pipeline and appraisal parameters
  • Climate and demographic projections
Implementation

From contract to live deployment

Implementation is scoped and priced separately from the subscription, because what it costs depends on the country’s data, the institutions involved and the model estate being stood up.

01Scoping Institutions, questions, model estate, data inventory, users and the governance that has to hold the deployment together.
02Country configuration Country setup, model calibration to national accounts and parameters, baseline validation against published outturns.
03Data integration National datasets mapped, loaded and versioned under the institution’s own data governance, with gaps recorded.
04Institutional setup Workspaces, users, roles, approval routes, retention and audit, matched to how the institution actually works.
05Integration APIs and connections to the systems the institution already runs, where those systems can expose the data.
06Training and certification Analysts trained on the models they will run and certified against the Academy register; administrators trained on the estate.
07Testing and acceptance Workflows run end to end on the institution’s own questions, with results reviewed before acceptance.
08Operation and review The institution runs its own analysis, with support, model updates and an annual review against new data and new policy.

Integration

EDIS joins the systems you already run

A deployment is only useful if it reads the institution’s real data and writes back into the processes that use it. EDIS exposes governed APIs for indicators, scenarios, runs and reports, and reads from the systems that can publish data to it.

  • Governed REST API for indicators, scenarios, runs and report retrieval
  • Budget and public financial management systems, where they expose data
  • Statistics-office data services and national data warehouses
  • Sector management information systems and administrative registers
  • GIS services and spatial data infrastructure
  • Identity and access management for institutional sign-in

What stays under your control

  • Your data stays in your organisation’s scope, with cross-organisation isolation
  • Role-based access, approval routes and a full audit trail
  • Configurable retention and export controls
  • No private dataset is exposed through the public site or the demonstration
  • Model versions are pinned, so a published result can be reproduced

Development partners

Partner with EDIS to build national decision intelligence

A deployment can be financed by a development partner, by the government, or shared between a donor, the government and AICTEM. Partner-supported deployments are scoped with the partner and the beneficiary institution together.

Donor support is best used to establish the infrastructure and the institutional capacity — country configuration, data integration, model development and certified analysts — so that the government can carry the platform afterwards. The funding model is designed to end in government ownership, not in permanent donor dependence.
