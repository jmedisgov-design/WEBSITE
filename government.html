EDIS for governments

National economic decision infrastructure

Governments do not buy a dashboard. They buy the capacity to answer, defensibly and in their own hands, what a policy costs, what it produces, who it reaches and whether the country can afford it. EDIS is that capacity: FINEX, MTEF, CDCGE, the Budget Output Engine, UCSN, public investment appraisal, the sector models, GIS and MEL, integrated and calibrated to one country.

Workflows, not feature lists. Each of these is a chain of models that starts with a question a real institution has to answer and ends with a decision it has to defend.

How a deployment runs

From first question to institutional capability

01Demonstration EDIS run against one of your own policy questions, with the model chain, the provenance and the limitations shown as they would be in production.
02Configuration Country setup, model calibration to your accounts and parameters, your data integrated, your users and roles provisioned.
03Training Your analysts trained on the models they will run, assessed and certified against the Academy register — not shown a demonstration.
04Operation and review The institution runs its own analysis, with priority support and an annual model review against new data and new policy.
Procurement

Built for public financial management

Government buyers cannot usually purchase with a credit card, and should not have to. EDIS institutional contracts support quotes, proforma invoices, formal proposals, purchase orders, framework agreements and development-partner procurement.

Where the country's income classification allows, development access pricing is applied at contract. Where a development partner is financing the deployment, we scope it with the partner and the beneficiary together.
