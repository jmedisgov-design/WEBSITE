The EDIS workflow

A policy question becomes a routed analytical workflow

EDIS never sends a policy question to a language model and returns the reply. The question is classified, the models are resolved, the data and permissions are checked, and only then is a workflow executed — in this order, every time.

01
Policy question In ordinary language, with country, region, horizon and currency.
02
AI agent and orchestrator Intent classified, country and sector identified, agents selected, the workflow and its execution order constructed.
03
Data and evidence Required datasets resolved, availability, vintage and permissions checked; gaps recorded before anything runs.
04
Economic models FINEX for the macro-fiscal frame, CDCGE for the economy-wide closure.
05
Sector models Energy, health, education, transport, agriculture, water and the rest, on a shared costing spine.
06
Fiscal and budget systems MTEF ceilings, the Budget Output Engine and unit costs: what the allocation buys.
07
Investment appraisal PIAS ranks and appraises the pipeline on a common basis.
08
GIS and spatial analysis Where it is needed, who it reaches, and what it costs to reach them.
09
Poverty and distributional analysis Microsimulation on household data: who gains, who loses, by how much.
10
Monitoring and evaluation Results mapped to indicators, targets and what was actually delivered.
11
Decision report A brief or technical report with charts, maps, risks, options and full provenance.

From a policy question to a decision

One question, ten models, one traceable answer

“What would happen to GDP, fiscal balances, employment, poverty and sector investment requirements if public investment in renewable energy increased over five years?”

Agent → FINEX → MTEF → Energy model → UCSN → Budget Output Engine → PIAS → CDCGE → Poverty microsimulation → MEL → Decision report

No single model answers that question. EDIS runs the chain, shows each model as it completes, and stops rather than guessing when a model does not converge or a dataset is missing. That cross-model workflow is what separates EDIS from a chatbot or a standalone model.

AI agents for economic decision intelligence

Agents make the models usable. They do not replace them.

The AI layer and the analytical layer are separated by design, and the separation is enforced in the architecture rather than merely described in the marketing. An agent routes, orchestrates, validates and explains; every number comes from a model.

  1. Understand — interpret the policy question and its scope
  2. Identify data — find the datasets that can support an answer
  3. Identify models — select the model services that accept the question
  4. Construct the workflow — order the chain and its dependencies
  5. Execute and coordinate — queue, run and monitor the models
  6. Validate — convergence, plausibility and consistency checks
  7. Interpret — explain what the results mean and what they do not
  8. Report — assemble the decision report with full provenance

Why EDIS

What makes it different

Integrated

Multiple models and systems in one platform, rather than a folder of disconnected spreadsheets and tools.

Evidence-based

Results are produced by data and analytical models, each traceable to a dataset and a model version.

Cross-sector

Links macroeconomics, fiscal policy and sector planning, so sector plans are costed against the envelope.

Spatial

GIS and geographic analysis are part of the answer: where it is needed and who it reaches.

Scenario-based

Policy simulation rather than static reporting: what changes, under which assumptions, by when.

AI-enabled

Agents help users navigate and orchestrate analytical workflows; the models still produce the numbers.

Government-oriented

Built around real planning, budgeting, investment and policy workflows, and procured the way governments procure.

Failure is shown

Non-converged runs and missing data are displayed, not hidden, because hiding them would misrepresent the evidence.
